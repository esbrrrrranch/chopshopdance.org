The CHOP SHOP 2021 Dance Film Festival, was a special online iteration of the festival co-designed by the outstanding participating artists! We premiered dance films created by the participating artists each Thursday in February on YouTube, with a live Zoom Q&A with the week’s artists each Sunday. The films remained accessible through 11.59pm PDT on Wednesday, March 31, 2021.

The 2021 CHOP SHOP Dance Film Festival featured films by:
Lauren Horn//Subira Vs. Movement (Windsor, CT)
Nicole von Arx / NVA & Guests (Brooklyn, NY & Switzerland)
Omar Román De Jesús / Boca Tuya (New York City, NY)

The 2021 CHOP SHOP Festival was made possible through the wonderful support of the City of Bellevue, 4Culture, Washington State Arts Commission, and the National Endowment for the Arts, along with all of the individual donors who have powered and sustained the festival over the past 14 years.

About the Films.

Thursday, February 4
World premieres from Lauren Horn and Nicole von Arx

@Me
Lauren Horn // Subira Vs. Movement (Windsor, CT)
@Me is a digital biography of the life of digital native Lauren Horn. Cascading through different lenses, it looks at online expression. From learning dance videos online to making a vlog to crafting the perfect Instagram post, it plays with the sense of isolation that comes with using the internet as your main mode of connecting with others and providing entertainment. Inspired by the Covid-19 pandemic, it explores the ways that many of us have delved deeper into our online lives during the quarantine.

Choreography, Performance, & Videography: Lauren Horn
Music: Patrick Greeley, New York and Sour; Nyokabi Kariuki, Pink Pillows

Thank you to my mom for always supporting my work. To Sam for being there for me in every moment of doubt. To Alice for listening to me ramble on about technology and work out all of my dance/technology feelings. To Deb for helping me figure out how to be friends with the camera.

Learn more about Lauren Horn and Subira Vs. Movement at subiravsmovement.com. Instagram: @subiravsmovement.

Bright Night
Nicole von Arx / NVA & Guests (Brooklyn / Switzerland)
Sometimes good, sometimes great, and sometimes not well at all. How do you feel these days? Bright Night is a short film created so that maybe you, me, we can think about those darker days. And more importantly, make them feel more real. So that the conversation around them can start. NOTE: Bright Night includes the use of a strobe effect.

Direction, Editing, & Choreography: Nicole von Arx
Cinematography: Dan Gross
Performance: Nicole von Arx
Sound Composition, Text, & Voice: Nicole von Arx
Music: Claude Debussy, Clair de Lune

Special thanks to Dan Gross for helping me film this on the very last day of 2020. And a huge thank you to CHOP SHOP for keeping us artists all on our creative feet.

Learn more about Nicole von Arx at nvonarx.com. Instagram: @nicolevarx / @nva.and.guests

Thursday, February 11
World premiere from Omar Román De Jesús

Los Perros del Barrio Colosal
Omar Román De Jesús / Boca Tuya (New York City)
Through the exaggerated mannerisms of daytime television, the six characters of Los Perros del Barrio Colosal take us on a wild romp through the challenges of creative decision making. Diving dramatically into the adventure of an imagination yet to be physicalized, they ask us to consider the far side of the moments when our ideas threaten, with disjointed urgency, to swallow us whole.

Choreography: Omar Román De Jesús
Videography: Drew L. Brown
Performance: Rafael Cañals, Carlos Falú Sanchez, Rachel Secrest, Ian Spring, Christian Warner, Spencer Weidie
Costumes: Omar Román De Jesús
Music: Lucho Bermúdez, Homenaje al Gran Lucho; Franz Liszt, Liebestruame, S541/R211: No. 3: Nocturne in A-Flat Major
Filmed at a Civil War-era warehouse via Peerspace.

Special thanks to Dolly Sfeir, Barbara Delo, and Joamer González. This film was made possible thanks to CHOP SHOP and the generosity of our supporters.

Learn more about Omar Román De Jesús and Boca Tuya at bocatuya.com. Instagram: @bocatuya____

Violet crumble
Eva Stone & Simone Elliott / Root Collective (Seattle / Germany)
This film begins at escape. It is conceived from the point of view of a woman finding a way to cope with the consequences of a traumatic event. From the moment her feet touch the ground, we find ourselves alongside her, entering a place that is safe from damage and pain. We are offered the sensations of sanctuary, yet beneath these still waters are the forces of a riptide of memory, recognition, and unattainable recovery.

Concept: Eva Stone
Choreography: Simone Elliott & Eva Stone
Cinematography & Editing: Péter Dániel Matkaicsek | MpD Movement Pictures
Performance: Simone Elliott
Music: Bryan Lineberry, Untitled 5

Thursday, February 25
World premieres from Elyse Mertz & Javier Padilla and Mark Haim

how to become an escape artist
Elyse Mertz & Javier Padilla (New York City)
how to become an escape artist is a dreamscape. It exhibits the subconscious of a person in constant flux. It shows us where we run to physically and emotionally, the things that we carry as we continuously escape, and the words that we keep hidden.

Choreography & Performance: Javier Padilla
Cinematography & Direction: Elyse Mertz
Music & Text: Javier Padilla

Learn more about Javier Padilla and The Movement Playground at jpmovementplayground.me. Instagram: @javipadilla26

WALDO: 2020
Mark Haim (Seattle)
Mark Haim writes of his dance film WALDO: 2020, “ In lockdown, quarantine, and self-imposed isolation during the pandemic, I found solace and company in plant life. Learning that trees communicate with each other, that they help each other out when things get tough, that they share resources instead of hoarding them, inspired me to envision my body holding a different sort of space in the world, in nature -- far less important or significant than what humanity has taught me to imagine. From there, I came up with the idea of re-creating the child's game called Find Waldo: find me hidden, folded in, blended with life.” Mark Haim’s WALDO: 2020 was filmed in the forests along the I-90 corridor, the lands of the Muckleshoot, Coast Samish, Duwamish, and Tulalip people.

Choreography, Performance, & Videography: Mark Haim

Special thanks to Hendri Walujo, Koushik Ghosh, and Lisa Kusanagi.

Learn more about Mark Haim at markhaimart.com.
